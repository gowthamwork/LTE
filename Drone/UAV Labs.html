Experiments to try

1 · Find the hover point. Press “Hover point”. How much throttle does the motor need just to lift ¼ of the drone? Now add weight with the slider — what happens to current and flight time?
2 · Bigger prop, same motor. Raise the propeller diameter from 10 to 13 in. Thrust goes up — but so does the current. Watch the motor temperature.
3 · Overdrive it. Click the “Overdriven” preset and push to 100 %. Watch current pass the red line, the motor glow, then smoke. Switch off Protection to let it burn.
4 · Drain a battery. Hold 70 % at time-lapse ×300. Watch voltage sag grow and thrust fall as the battery empties, until the low-voltage cut-off.
5 · Efficiency sweet spot. Run the ramp test and watch the efficiency chart (g/W). Why is a drone most efficient at low throttle?
6 · KV vs prop size. Compare 2300 KV + 5 in against 400 KV + 13 in at the same thrust. Which draws fewer watts?

What to notice

Bigger battery ≠ always longer. Sweep capacity: flight time rises, then flattens, then falls, because the extra battery is extra weight that must also be lifted.
Bigger props win efficiency. Sweep prop diameter: a large slow prop moves more air per watt. But watch the red zone — motors that are too small for the load overheat.
KV must match the prop. Sweep KV: too low and it can’t lift off; too high and the motor is overdriven (current line crosses the rating).
Payload costs more than its weight. Add 1 kg payload: hover power rises faster than weight because power ∝ thrust^1.5.

Motion:

Scoreboard — which estimate is closest to the truth?

Gyroscope

Measures how fast the drone rotates. Add up (integrate) the rotation rate and you get the angle. It is very smooth, but any tiny constant error (bias) is added up too, so the angle drifts away forever.
angle = angle + gyro × dt

Accelerometer

Feels gravity, so the direction of “down” tells the tilt. It never drifts, but it is noisy, shaken by motor vibration, and fooled whenever the drone itself accelerates (it cannot tell gravity from a push).
angle = atan2(ax, az)

Fusion

Trust the gyro for fast changes and the accelerometer for the long-term average. Complementary filter: a simple blend. Kalman filter: also learns the gyro bias and weighs each sensor by how noisy it is.
angle = α·(angle + gyro·dt) + (1−α)·accel

How to think about P, I and D

P — Proportional (“how far off am I?”)
Pushes harder the bigger the error. Too small: lazy, never gets there. Too big: overshoots and oscillates, like a spring with no damper.
I — Integral (“how long have I been off?”)
Adds up past error so a steady push (imbalance, wind, a heavy payload) is cancelled completely. Too big: slow wobble and overshoot (“windup”).
D — Derivative (“how fast am I moving?”)
Brakes the motion, so it arrives without overshooting, like a damper. Too big: it amplifies sensor noise and the motors buzz.

Why it works (or doesn’t)

Four numbers to control. Total lift, roll torque, pitch torque and yaw torque. Each motor adds a column to a small table (the “mixer”). With 4 motors there is exactly one answer; if one dies, you lose a degree of freedom.
Quad: sacrifice the yaw. After a failure the only way to keep lift, roll and pitch balanced is to use the two diagonal motors. They spin the same way, so the drone pirouettes. It needs a big thrust reserve and detection within a few milliseconds. Research quads have done it; commercial ones almost never do.
Hex / octo: spare capacity. More motors give more ways to make the same four numbers, so the allocator can re-balance and keep yaw too. The price is weight, cost and battery draw. Coaxial X8 keeps the footprint of a quad.

Flight log (60 s) — hover to inspect

A systematic way to read any log

1 · Find the moment it went wrong. Look for the first plot that leaves its normal band. Faults have a start time; causes come before effects.
2 · Ask “command or response?” If the drone did not follow its command (attitude plot), look at the motors. If the command itself was wrong, look at sensors (heading, GPS, vibration).
3 · Check the unglamorous plots. Battery voltage under load, vibration and GPS quality explain a huge share of real crashes long before anyone suspects the code.

Fault signatures cheat-sheet

Weak or damaged motor/prop: one motor output sits well above the others and yaw slowly drifts.
Vibration: accelerometer vibration far above 30 m/s², altitude estimate jittery, all motors noisy.
Bad PID tune: roll oscillates around the command, motor outputs swap up/down at a steady rhythm.
Weak battery: voltage collapses under current, every motor works harder as it drops, altitude finally sags.
Compass interference: compass heading and gyro heading disagree when current is high; the position circles.
GPS glitch: satellites fall and HDOP rises just before a sudden position jump and a lurch.
